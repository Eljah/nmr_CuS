                                                                                            most active mineral is bornite and the noblest minerals are pyrite
                                                                                            and chalcopyrite (Mehta and Murr, 1983; Nowak et al., 1984;
                                                                                            Chmielewski and Kaleta, 2011). The nobler sulﬁdes can increase

                                                                                            netic factors can be more important for the galvanic effect than
                                                                                            the potential difference (Holmes and Crundwell, 1995). In the
                                                                                            case of CuFeS2:FeS2 pair dissolution rate increases with increasing
                                                                                            amount of the nobler pyrite. However, if the amount of pyrite is
                                                                                            less than that of chalcopyrite the increase is small, and signiﬁcant
                                                                                            increase is seen only at equal amounts (Mehta and Murr, 1983).
                                                                                            The potential difference between chalcopyrite and pyrite is small
                                                                                            and does not cause much galvanic effect (Nowak et al., 1984).

    The leaching experiments showed that in the studied cupric chlo-
ride environment iron did not dissolve from pyrite (Fig. 5). Pyrite
concentrate from Pyhäsalmi was very pure (99.8% pyrite) having
45% of iron (Table 1). As shown in Table 2, after the dissolution
tests the solution had 94 mg/L iron, which means that only 1.5% of
iron from pyrite concentrate dissolved.
    Also iron in hematite and magnetite were shown to be resistant
for leaching in cupric chloride solution. Salobo concentrate had ca.
20% iron, which was mostly included in magnetite and hematite
(Fig. 2). After 2 h of leaching there was 62 mg/L iron in the solution
indicating that only 1.8% of iron from the Salobo concentrate dis-
solved (Fig. 5). As the weight ratio of copper and iron in bornite is
5.68 the low amount of iron in the solution indicates precipitation
of iron.

    In the studied environment, only a minor amount of iron was
dissolved from pyrite, magnetite or hematite. Soluble iron analyzed in
the solution originated from copper sulﬁdes, such as chalcopyrite and
bornite. The dissolution of Gibraltar concentrate produced more iron
that would theoretically come from chalcopyrite dissolution. This
indicates partial dissolution of iron or back precipitation of copper.

4.3. Dissolution rates of the concentrates

dissolution rates of the concentrates. According to the mixed poten-
tial theory the charge consumed in reduction of the oxidant must be
balanced by oxidation of the concentrate. The reduction of the oxi-
dant can be estimated from the Nernst Eq. (5) as the decrease in
redox potential. The faster is the redox potential decrease during
the experiment the faster is the oxidation. From the redox potential
measurements it is possible to estimate two factors: the change of
redox potential that describes how far the concentrate dissolution
